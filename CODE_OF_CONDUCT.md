# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

We are committed to making participation in the **Federated Learning on Non-IID Data with Differential Privacy** project a respectful, inclusive, professional, and harassment-free experience for everyone.

This commitment applies regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, sexual identity or orientation, socioeconomic status, education, or technical background.

The project welcomes researchers, students, engineers, reviewers, maintainers, documentation contributors, security researchers, and users who engage constructively and in good faith.

## Expected Behavior

Community members are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.
- Give technical feedback that is specific, evidence-based, and focused on the work.
- Accept constructive criticism without personal attacks.
- Respect differences in experience, language, research background, and technical opinion.
- Clearly distinguish verified results from assumptions, simulations, hypotheses, and future work.
- Credit prior work, external research, datasets, implementations, and contributors appropriately.
- Protect private information, credentials, unpublished vulnerability details, and sensitive research material.
- Use project spaces for collaboration, learning, engineering, reproducibility, and responsible research.
- Follow repository contribution, security, licensing, and validation requirements.
- Help create an environment where questions and mistakes can be discussed without humiliation.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking, threats, or discriminatory conduct.
- Insults, personal attacks, deliberate humiliation, or hostile communication.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.
- Knowingly submitting fabricated benchmarks, manipulated results, false privacy claims, or misleading validation claims.
- Presenting simulated, local, or incomplete evidence as production certification or real-world deployment validation.
- Plagiarism, removal of required attribution, or misrepresentation of authorship.
- Deliberately introducing malware, backdoors, credential theft, destructive code, or undisclosed telemetry.
- Publicly disclosing an unpatched security vulnerability before the maintainer has had a reasonable opportunity to investigate and respond.
- Repeatedly disrupting discussions, pull requests, reviews, issue tracking, or project collaboration.
- Retaliating against someone who reports a conduct or security concern in good faith.

## Research and Technical Integrity

Because this repository contains federated learning, differential privacy, security, and distributed-systems components, contributors must use careful and accurate language.

In particular:

- Differential privacy values must be reported with their threat model, sampling assumptions, clipping configuration, noise mechanism, privacy accountant, number of rounds, and target delta.
- Experimental results must identify the dataset, partition strategy, random seed policy, algorithm configuration, hardware or runtime assumptions, and relevant limitations.
- Auxiliary components must not be described as active in the default runtime unless they are actually integrated and validated there.
- A passing CI workflow must not be presented as formal security certification, regulatory compliance, production readiness, or proof of complete adversarial resistance.
- Security-sensitive claims must be supported by tests, implementation traceability, and clearly stated limitations.

Deliberate technical misrepresentation may be treated as a conduct violation.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces, including:

- GitHub issues and discussions
- Pull requests and code reviews
- Commit messages
- Documentation and examples
- Community chat or collaboration channels
- Conferences, presentations, demonstrations, or publications that officially represent the project
- Private communication related to project participation

It also applies when a person is publicly representing the project or acting in an official project role.

## Reporting a Conduct Concern

Do not report sensitive conduct concerns in a public GitHub issue.

Report the concern privately to the project maintainer through the maintainer's GitHub profile or another private contact method explicitly published by the maintainer.

A useful report should include:

- A clear description of what happened
- Where and when it occurred
- Links, screenshots, or other relevant evidence
- The names or GitHub usernames involved
- Whether the behavior is continuing
- Any immediate safety or privacy concern

Reports made in good faith will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible. Information may be shared only when necessary to investigate, protect participants, comply with law, or take corrective action.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct.

Depending on severity, context, history, and impact, corrective actions may include:

1. A private clarification or informal warning
2. A formal warning with required corrective action
3. Removal or editing of comments, issues, pull requests, or other content
4. Temporary restriction from project interaction
5. Rejection of contributions
6. Permanent removal from project spaces
7. Escalation to GitHub or relevant authorities when necessary

Maintainers should avoid conflicts of interest. When a report concerns a maintainer, another trusted reviewer should handle the report where reasonably possible.

## No Retaliation

Retaliation against a person who raises a concern, provides evidence, participates in an investigation, or requests enforcement in good faith is prohibited.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may also result in corrective action.

## Good-Faith Disagreement

Technical disagreement is normal and valuable. Challenging an implementation, mathematical assumption, benchmark, design choice, privacy analysis, or security claim is not harassment when done respectfully and with evidence.

Community members should separate criticism of ideas and code from criticism of people.
